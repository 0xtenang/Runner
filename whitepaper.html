1Summary

RUNNER is a running app first: live GPS tracking, pace, splits and heart rate, free for everyone. On top of it sits an optional run-to-earn layer:

  • RUNNER Genesis — 9,999 sneaker NFTs. Owning one lets you earn.
  • RUNNER token — a fixed supply of 1,000,000,000. 80% is reserved for runners and released on a halving schedule.
  • Verified runs — every run is re-checked by a server with the same rules as the app, plus fair-play checks, before it earns anything.
Status: this is a draft. The smart contracts are written, tested and reviewed internally, but not deployed and not yet audited by an independent firm. Numbers below are the current design and may change before launch. Nothing here is investment advice or a promise of value.

2The app

Available at runearn.fun and as Android and iPhone apps. Sign in with Google, Apple or email and a Robinhood Chain wallet is created for you (via Privy) — no seed phrase to manage. Guests can track runs without signing in.

  • Record runs with a live map, distance, pace, splits, elevation and heart rate (Bluetooth sensors supported).
  • Only runs recorded live with GPS in the app earn.
  • The Earn tab shows your sneakers, energy, balance, claims, holder rewards and the current emission.

Earn rate

RUNNER per minute = 112.5 × Efficiency × class rate × emission factor × NFT boost
(+10% with a live heart-rate sensor)

The emission factor is today's emission ÷ 10,000,000 — 100% at launch, then it halves with the emission schedule (section 6). NFT boosts come from future collections (up to +100% in total). Example for one sneaker, month 1, 30 minutes: a typical Common earns about 20,000 RUNNER, an Epic about 118,000.

Levels, attributes and durability

  • Levels 0–30. A level-up costs 1,100 × (next level)^1.5 × emission factor RUNNER — about 1,100 for level 1, 35,000 for level 10, 181,000 for level 30 at launch prices. Each level gives attribute points (Common 1, Uncommon 1, Rare 2, Epic 2) for Efficiency (more RUNNER) or Resilience (less wear). A Common that reinvests its earnings reaches level 30 in about a month.
  • Durability drops about 5.4 per energy for a typical Common (≈9 days of daily runs to 50%). Below 50, Efficiency is reduced (down to 20%). Repairs cost 75 RUNNER per point × (1 + 10% per level) × emission factor — about 2% of what the sneaker earns between repairs. Sneakers never break or disappear.
  • RUNNER spent on level-ups and repairs is never paid out — it stays in the run-reward pool.

6Emission & claims

Halving emission

The run-reward pool releases at most a set amount per UTC day. The launch schedule: 10,000,000 RUNNER per day, halving every 30 days, never below 100,000 per day. It starts on launch day, when the dev wallet starts it on-chain.

If every day's maximum were used, about 38% of the pool would be released in month 1, 66% by month 3 and 75% by month 7, after which 100,000 per day continues for about 5.5 years. In practice runners only earn what energy, pace zones and fair-play checks allow; with 100 active runners, about 2.9M RUNNER per day would be emitted in month 1.

The dev wallet can set a new schedule (daily amount, halving period, floor). A change never affects the current day — it starts the next UTC day — and can never exceed 20,000,000 per day. Runners' earn rates follow the schedule automatically.

From earnings to tokens

  1. Finish a run. The server checks it and credits your in-app balance. New earnings become usable 24 hours after the run.
  2. Spend in the app, or claim to your wallet. The server signs your claim; your wallet submits it to the token contract.
  3. Choose: Lock 7 days → 100%, or Now → 80% to you, 20% burned. Locked rewards can be released after 7 days (one transaction a week is enough), or taken early at 80%. Only you can choose "now" for your own rewards.

Claims across all runners can never exceed the day's emission; if it's nearly used up, you get a partial claim and the rest the next day — nothing is lost.

7Trading tax & holder rewards

  • 4% tax on buys and sells through registered exchange pools, fixed forever in the contract: 2% to the protocol wallet and 2% to holders. Wallet-to-wallet transfers and run-reward claims are not taxed.
  • Holder rewards: each UTC day's 2% is shared by every wallet that held at least 300,000 RUNNER for the whole day, in proportion to the lowest balance it held that day. Buying just before the day ends doesn't count — you need to hold through a full day.
  • Each day's share can be claimed for 7 days; after that, anything unclaimed goes to the protocol wallet.
  • Exchange pools and the token contract itself get no holder rewards; dev and team wallets are eligible like any holder.
Because of the tax, use exchanges that support fee-on-transfer tokens (Uniswap-V2-style pools) and expect to set about 5% slippage.

8Fair play

No run-to-earn app is completely cheat-proof. RUNNER aims to make cheating cost more than it pays, decided by rules rather than people:

  • The server decides, not the phone. Every uploaded GPS track is re-scored with the same rules as the app. Sneaker ownership is read from the chain; replayed or overlapping runs, and one sneaker used by two wallets at once, are rejected.
  • Trust score (0–100). Real GPS is messy; scripts and spoofers tend to be too clean. Points are lost for perfectly regular fix timing, constant speed, identical segments, perfectly straight routes, unchanging GPS accuracy, a fake-GPS app, steps that don't match the pace, or a route copied from another upload.
  • Phone app checks. The phone apps send the step counter (bikes, scooters and cars give almost no steps) and a device proof (Google Play Integrity / Apple App Attest) showing the run came from the genuine app on an unmodified phone. Real-token earning can be limited to the phone apps.
Trust scoreOutcome
70–100Paid in full
40–6950% paid, no penalty
Under 40, fake-GPS app, or copied routeNot paid + 1 strike

Three strikes within 30 days suspend the wallet automatically — 7 days, then 30 days, then permanently — and cancel its earnings still in the 24-hour hold. Strikes expire after 30 days. Requests are also rate-limited, and per-wallet energy plus the daily emission cap how much any single wallet can earn.

Nobody — including the owner — can: change the 4% tax, mint new tokens, exceed 20M RUNNER of emission per day, change today's emission, or take tokens out of the run-reward pool, locked claims or the holder-reward bucket.

Before launch, ownership is planned to move to a multisig wallet so no single key controls these settings.

10Security & audits

  • Internal review of both contracts and the rewards server, with findings, fixes and a full list of owner powers in AUDIT.md. It was done by the same AI-assisted process that wrote the code, so it is not a substitute for an independent audit.
  • Automated tests: 278 checks across the token, the NFT and the rewards server, including a randomized 45-day check of the holder-reward bookkeeping and deliberately planted bugs to prove the tests catch them. Static analysis with Slither found no outstanding issues.
  • Independent audit: planned with IMD before any mainnet deployment.
  • Verifiable builds: the deploy page refuses to deploy any contract whose bytecode differs from the reviewed build. Source code is public at github.com/0xtenang/Runner.

11Roadmap

Order, not dates — each step starts when the previous one is ready.

  1. Now — open beta. Run tracking live at runearn.fun; earning in local test mode; contracts written and internally reviewed.
  2. Audit & hardening. Independent audit (IMD), multisig ownership, phone apps in the stores with device checks.
  3. Launch. Deploy the NFT and token, open the sneaker sale, add liquidity, register the pool, start emission.
  4. After launch. Watch auto-sync (Garmin and others), future sneaker collections with earning boosts, more ways to use RUNNER in the app.

12Risks

  • No guaranteed value. RUNNER and the sneakers may lose value or become illiquid. Earnings in dollars depend on the market price, which nobody controls. At a low market cap, daily earnings are worth only cents.
  • Front-loaded emission. Most rewards can be released in the first months, which can create heavy selling pressure.
  • Smart-contract risk. Bugs are possible despite reviews and tests. Contracts are not upgradeable.
  • Centralization. The dev wallet holds the controls in section 9 until ownership moves to a multisig; the rewards server decides which runs earn.
  • Cheating. Fair-play checks reduce but cannot eliminate cheating.
  • Thin early liquidity means large price swings, made larger by the 4% tax.
  • Regulation. Rules for tokens differ by country (in Indonesia, for example, OJK oversight applies). Check what applies to you.
This document describes software and a game economy. It is not an offer to sell securities, not financial advice and not a promise of returns.
